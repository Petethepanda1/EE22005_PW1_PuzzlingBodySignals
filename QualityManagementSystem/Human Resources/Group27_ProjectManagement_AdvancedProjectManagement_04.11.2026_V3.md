# Project Aims
The aim of this project is to investigate how electromyography signals can be acquired with a kit, processed and analysed to better understand the anatomy of the human muscle. We aim to evaluate bio signals acquired through different methods, develop a reliable EMG-based system and assess our results through practical experiments. The project also aims to strengthen our technical, problem-solving, teamwork and communication skills through effective project management, efficient task allocation will help with managing all aspects of the project while maximising hands-on experience with the EMG equipment when received.
# Project Scope
This project is to be completed within the lab time allocated during week 6 with a deadline of Friday 6th 14:15. We have been provided with an EMG sensor, 12 disposable electrode pads and have been instructed to use the Arduino nano ESP32 provided to us in Year 1. The end goal of this project is to be able to answer the question: How hard do our muscles work during day–to–day activities? 
# Project Objectives
### **Objective 1: Project Management and Organisation**
By the end of Day 1, we established a shared Microsoft Teams workspace, completed a skills matrix and allocated responsibilities based on team members' competencies and preferences. Furthermore, we developed a project schedule with defined deadlines and milestones to ensure effective communication, organisation and coordination throughout the week.
### **Objective 2: EMG System Configuration**
By the end of Day 2, collect, assemble and configure the available EMG equipment, including soldering if necessary. Verify system functionality by recording at least one usable muscle activity signal during a controlled arm movement, ensuring experimental data collection is consistent through repetition of experiment and the use of the same individual. 
### **Objective 3: EMG Data Acquisition**
By the end of Day 3, establish a standardised arm movement and acquire at least a valid electromyography signal recording under consistent experimental conditions. Assess signal quality, identify potential measurement errors and organise the collected data into clearly labelled datasets for MATLAB processing and analysis.
### **Objective 4: Signal Processing and Analysis**
By the end of day 4, develop a MATLAB code to import our data, filter and analyse the recorded signals. Generate at least a labelled graph illustrating raw and processed signals, comparing muscle activation characteristics across tests and evaluate signal consistency, potential noise sources and measurement limitations.
### **Objective 5: Scientific Communication** 
From morning until 11am of day 5, produce a final project presentation and scientific communication video explaining the system, experimental methodology and key findings. Communicate results effectively to technical and non-technical audiences using relevant graphs, clear explanations and justified engineering decisions, while highlighting project limitations. Make use of the end of day 5 to finish portfolio.

# Work Packages
### **WP1: Project Management and Communication**
**Task 1.1: Communication and File Organisation**

Create a shared Microsoft Teams workspace with clearly labelled folders for planning documents, experimental data, MATLAB scripts and presentation materials. Establish a consistent file-naming system to ensure accessibility.

**Task 1.2: Skills Matrix and Task Allocation**

Develop a skills matrix assessing each team member's technical abilities, experience and preferred responsibilities. Allocate tasks according to competence levels and interests while providing opportunities to develop new skills.

**Task 1.3: Time and Project Management**

Create a project schedule identifying task dependencies, deadlines and milestones. Monitor progress through Teams and update task allocations when necessary to ensure completion within our timeline.

**Deliverables:**

- D1: Shared Microsoft Teams workspace with organised documentation.

- D2: Completed skills matrix and task allocation.

- D3: Project schedule and milestone tracker.

**Milestone M1: Project Planning Completed**

Shared workspace established, skills matrix completed, responsibilities allocated and project schedule agreed upon by all team members.

### **WP2: EMG Signal Acquisition**
**Task 2.1: Equipment Collection and Assembly**

Collect and inspect the EMG kit, electrodes, cables and acquisition hardware. Assemble the required connections and perform soldering if necessary, ensuring the system is electrically safe and functional.

**Task 2.2: EMG System Configuration**

Position electrodes on the selected arm muscle and configure the acquisition system. Check signal quality, sampling settings and connectivity before recording experimental data.

**Task 2.3: Experimental Procedure**

Develop an arm movement that will be repeated during a specific duration. Position electrodes so that testing conditions are respected. Perform repeated trials to obtain comparable EMG signals.

**Task 2.4: Data Collection and Validation**

Record at least five trials of the selected movement. Inspect recordings for noise, missing data and inconsistencies. Save and label valid measurements for MATLAB processing.

**Deliverables:**

- D4: Functional and tested EMG acquisition system.

- D5: Documented experimental procedure and labelled EMG data.

**Milestone M2: EMG System Operational**

EMG equipment successfully assembled, configured and tested, with a usable muscle activity signal recorded.

**Milestone M3: Experimental Data Collected**

At least five valid EMG recordings successfully acquired, verified and organised for subsequent processing.

### **WP3: Signal Processing and Analysis**
**Task 3.1: Data Import and Organisation**

Import recorded EMG data into MATLAB and organise the measurements into labelled datasets. Verify sampling information and plot the raw signals to identify potential acquisition problems.

**Task 3.2: Signal Filtering**

Investigate suitable filtering techniques to reduce unwanted noise and interference. Implement appropriate filters in MATLAB and compare the raw and filtered signals to evaluate processing effectiveness.

**Task 3.3: Signal Analysis**

Analyse EMG characteristics such as amplitude, root mean square values. Compare repeated trials to investigate consistency and variations in muscle activity.

**Task 3.4: Results Interpretation**

Generate labelled plots and summarise the main findings. Discuss measurement reliability, possible sources of error and limitations of the acquisition and processing methods.

**Deliverables:**

- D6: Functional MATLAB scripts for signal filtering and analysis.

- D7: Raw and processed EMG graphs with interpreted results.

**Milestone M4: Signal Analysis Completed**

MATLAB processing successfully implemented, relevant signal characteristics evaluated and experimental findings supported by graphical evidence.

### **WP4: Scientific Communication and Project Presentation**
**Task 4.1: Results Compilation**

Collect relevant experimental data, MATLAB plots and engineering decisions from previous work packages. Organise the information into a clear narrative highlighting the project's objectives, methodology and findings.

**Task 4.2: Final Presentation**

Develop a presentation explaining the EMG acquisition system, experimental procedure, signal processing and results. Include diagrams, graphs and conclusions to support understanding.

**Task 4.3: Scientific Communication Video**

Produce a short educational video demonstrating the EMG system and explaining how muscle activity generates measurable electrical signals. Adapt the explanations for audiences without an engineering background.

**Task 4.4: Project Review and Reflection**

Evaluate the project's achievements against its SMART objectives. Identify technical limitations, potential improvements and lessons learned regarding teamwork, project management and experimental methods.

**Deliverables:**

- D8: Final project presentation.

- D9: Scientific communication video.

- D10: Project evaluation and reflection.

**Milestone M5: Final Project Delivery**

Final presentation and scientific communication video completed and delivered, with project outcomes evaluated against the initial objectives.
